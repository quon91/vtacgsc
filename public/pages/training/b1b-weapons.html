Weapons Employment

Bay loadouts, stores management, target designation, release criteria and safe escape. Phase 3 material — this is what Surface Attack Certification is graded on.

💣
What this qualification covers

The B-1B carries the largest internal payload in the inventory across three weapons bays. This guide covers configuring the stores management system, designating a target, meeting release criteria, and flying a safe escape. Surface Attack Certification is graded on time-on-target accuracy — the weapon has to arrive inside a thirty-second window.

  • Prerequisite: Low-Level Navigation Qualification complete
  • Grading standard: TOT ±30 seconds, safe escape flown
  • Awards: SAT CERT toward B-1B CMR
Before anything else: weapons behaviour differs between B-1B add-ons and between MSFS 2020 and 2024. Confirm which build your squadron is flying before a weapons sortie — half of all "the bombs didn't release" reports trace back to a version mismatch, not a procedure error.
📦
The three weapons bays
Forward bay
Ahead of the wing box. Often carries the auxiliary fuel tank on long-range profiles instead of stores.
Intermediate bay
Between the forward bay and the wing carry-through. Standard rotary launcher position.
Aft bay
Behind the wing box. Watch the CG effect as this one empties.

Each bay takes a rotary launcher. Release order matters for centre of gravity — dropping an entire bay in one pass shifts the CG noticeably.

Stores management setup
  1. Confirm the loadout. Check what's actually in each bay before you plan the attack, not on the run-in.
  2. Select the bay. Choose which bay you're releasing from this pass.
  3. Select the station. Pick individual stations or the full rotary depending on your release plan.
  4. Set release mode. Single, pairs, or ripple. Ripple needs an interval set as well.
  5. Set the interval. For sticks, the spacing between weapons determines your impact pattern length.
  6. Load target coordinates. For JDAMs, enter target lat/long and elevation. An error here puts the weapon exactly where you told it to go, which is the problem.
  7. Arm the system. Master arm on, and confirm you have a ready indication before the IP.
Do this before the initial point, never after. The run-in from IP to target is not the time to be paging through stores management. Have it configured, armed and checked with several minutes to spare.
▶
IP to target
  1. Cross the initial point on time. Your TOT is built from this. Cross late and you are already fighting the clock.
  2. Check timing. Compare planned versus actual. Correct with small speed changes now — not with a large one at the end.
  3. Confirm the target designation. Verify the coordinates or the designation one last time.
  4. Verify master arm and ready. Last chance to catch a configuration problem.
  5. Open the bay doors. Doors take time to travel — do this before you need them, not at release.
  6. Hold the release parameters. Wings level, altitude and speed stable through release.
  7. Release. Confirm the weapons actually left — check the stores count, don't assume.
  8. Close the doors and fly the safe escape. Immediately.
⏱
Time on target

TOT is the graded element. Check your timing at every route point, not just at the IP. A five-second drift caught early is a two-knot correction; the same drift caught at the IP is a scramble you probably lose.

  • Early: reduce speed, or extend with a small dogleg if you have room
  • Late: increase speed within limits — check you have the fuel for it
  • Badly late: call it. A missed TOT briefed honestly beats a rushed unsafe run.
Safe escape: after release, turn away from the target and accelerate. Fragmentation from your own weapons is a real hazard at low level. Brief the escape heading and the turn direction before the flight — the run-in is not the time to decide which way you're going.

Common student errors
  • Configuring stores on the run-in. Coach: "Set it before the IP or you're not ready to fly this."
  • Late timing correction. Coach: "Check your time now — at every point, not just the IP."
  • Doors opened at release. Coach: "Doors take time. Open them early."
  • Assuming release worked. Coach: "Check your stores count. Did they actually go?"
  • Escape flown toward the target. Coach: "Which way are you turning? Say it before we launch."
  • Bad JDAM coordinates. Coach: "Read the target coordinates back to me before the IP."
  • Descending below the floor to fix timing. Coach: "Speed fixes timing. Altitude is a hard floor."
🎓
IP pre-brief checklist
  • Confirm the add-on and sim version before any weapons event
  • Student reads back target coordinates and elevation before stepping to the aircraft
  • Student states the TOT window and their time-check plan
  • Safe escape heading and turn direction briefed and understood
  • Abort criteria briefed — weather, terrain, unrecoverable timing
  • Student states release mode and interval, and why they chose it
